Take action: what you can do, and what's working elsewhere

The big decisions about our community — what we pay, what we get, and where the township goes next — don't just happen to you. This is a plain, sourced look at what other communities are doing about the same pressures, and what's in your hands as a resident. It's information, not advice or endorsement.

What other communities do

Real options other municipalities use. Sources linked. We're not saying which one Ramara should choose, just showing what's out there. See the full data and analysis behind these options →

Relief for those who need it most
Ontario requires upper-tier municipalities, like the County of Simcoe, to offer property-tax relief (deferring or cancelling increases) for low-income seniors and low-income persons with disabilities. Some municipalities also offer water-bill deferral or rebates, for example Toronto's relief and rebate programs and Ottawa's water-utility deferral.
Sources: City of Toronto · City of Ottawa · Ontario deferral application. Check the County of Simcoe for our area's program (Service Simcoe, 705-722-3132).
Chase senior-government dollars
Grants shift costs off local ratepayers. Ramara's own $30M came from the provincial MHIP program. Small and rural municipalities also draw on the Ontario Community Infrastructure Fund (OCIF) and the Housing-Enabling Water Systems Fund.
Sources: Township $30M grant release · Ontario Community Infrastructure Fund (OCIF).
Plan ahead so rates don't shock
Well-run utilities build reserve funds and asset-management plans that spread big replacement costs over time, instead of letting a failure spike everyone's bill at once.
Source: Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) water and wastewater materials.
Bring residents in early
Some municipalities run public engagement on their rate studies before the rates are set, so the community helps shape the plan. Collingwood, for instance, ran an open water-rate consultation.
Source: Engage Collingwood — water rates.
Share the load
Some municipalities study regional or shared-service models, or a municipal services corporation, to spread fixed costs across more users. It's an option under study elsewhere, with real trade-offs, not a recommendation.
Source: AMO / Ontario water-and-wastewater expert-panel report.

What's in your hands right now

Practical ways to be heard, today.

Know the facts first
Start with the rates & affordability brief, the wastewater brief and Ramara's Money — the township's own numbers, plainly laid out, so you can speak from facts.
Speak to the fall rate review
A comprehensive water and wastewater rate review comes back to council this fall. Residents can address council directly through a deputation or the open forum.
Link: the Township's deputation and open-forum request forms.
Ask for the records
Anyone can file a records request (MFIPPA) with the Clerk for the rate studies and the numbers behind the bills. If something isn't published, this is how you get it.
Watch what's coming
Subscribe to council agendas so changes aren't a surprise — the early warning is usually there months ahead.
Link: the Township's CivicWeb agenda subscription.
Use your vote
The October 26, 2026 municipal election is when the whole community sets the direction on costs and priorities.
Find your neighbours
Ratepayer and community associations give residents a shared, stronger voice than going it alone.

Share an idea, or stay in the loop

Seen something work in another community? Tell us — it might end up on this page. (We collect ideas and interests, not votes.)
