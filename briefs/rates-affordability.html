Rates & affordability: how Ramara compares

Last verified June 14, 2026 · Every claim links to its source · Jump to sources

The short version

Ramara's 2026 water rate sits mid-pack among comparable Ontario townships; its wastewater usage rate ($5.00/m³) is the highest of the ones we could confirm, and on the current schedule it rises about 13% a year. Provincial rules require these systems to pay for themselves through user fees, which is why small townships everywhere are raising rates. Other communities use a real toolkit — relief programs, grant-chasing, reserve planning, public input — to soften the blow.

Why rates keep climbing

Ontario expects water and wastewater systems to be paid for by the people who use them, not from property taxes — so small systems with few users see big fixed costs land hard on each household. Multi-year rate studies forecast a decade of costs and set annual increases (Severn's 2026–2035 study plans ~3.5%/yr water and ~4.5%/yr wastewater; Kawartha Lakes is applying 4% in 2026), and provincial law (O. Reg. 588/17) requires every municipality to keep a 10-year asset-management strategy. [Ontario: asset management (O. Reg. 588/17)] [Severn rate study, Watson & Associates] [City of Kawartha Lakes 2026 budget]

The toolkit other communities use

No recommendation — just the options comparable Ontario communities draw on:

Relief for those who need it

Ontario's Municipal Act (s.319) requires upper-tier municipalities to offer tax-increase relief for low-income seniors and persons with disabilities. For Ramara that's the County of Simcoe (reported up to ~$250/yr; confirm terms with Service Simcoe, 705-722-3132). Toronto and Ottawa also run water-bill relief.

Municipal Act s.319; City of Toronto; City of Ottawa.

Chasing senior-government dollars

Grants shift capital off ratepayers. Ramara's $30M from Ontario's Municipal Housing Infrastructure Program is its largest grant ever. Others use OCIF, the Housing-Enabling Water Systems Fund, and ICIP Green.

Township: $30M grant; Ontario: OCIF.

Planning so rates don't shock

Reserve funds and asset-management plans spread big replacement costs over time instead of letting a failure spike every bill at once. Kawartha Lakes is building a multi-million-dollar reserve while keeping increases steady.

City of Kawartha Lakes 2026 budget & rate study.

Bringing residents in early

Some municipalities consult before setting rates. Prince Edward County put four rate scenarios to residents; Collingwood and Ottawa ran engagement on fixed-vs-usage charges. A comprehensive Ramara rate review is due at council this fall.

Have Your Say Prince Edward County; Engage Collingwood; Engage Ottawa.

Shared services (with limits)

Some study a municipal services corporation to spread fixed costs across more users (Innisfil's InnServices, Frontenac MSC). A 2025 expert panel cautions this "can't guarantee lower user rates" and rates may rise short-term; savings come mostly from shared admin, not the plants.

LAS Water & Wastewater Expert Panel Report, Jan 2025; AMO/MFOA, June 2024.
