Voter turnout in Ramara: where we stand

Last verified July 16, 2026 · Every claim links to its source · Jump to sources

The short version

Ramara's municipality-wide voter turnout fell from 38.97% in 2018 to 36.08% in 2022. Among Ontario local municipalities with fewer than 30,000 residents, Ramara ranked 188th of 329 that reported ballots in 2022: close to the middle, but 1.32 percentage points below the group median. In a stricter comparison limited to municipalities where the mayor or reeve was elected rather than acclaimed in both years, Ramara ranked 119th of 161. What this measures: Ontario's files report participation across the whole municipal election. They do not publish a separate turnout denominator for the mayor ballot. This brief therefore says municipal turnout, not "turnout for mayor."

Turnout fell across most of the group

Of the 314 small municipalities with comparable, non-zero participation in both elections, 69.7% recorded lower turnout in 2022. Their unweighted average change was −4.93 percentage points. Ramara's decline was smaller at −2.89 points, which helps explain why its relative rank improved from 212th to 188th even though fewer eligible electors voted. This is descriptive evidence: the source files do not explain why turnout changed. [AMO 2022 election analysis] [Ontario results]

How the comparison was built

The starting universe is Ontario's 414 lower- and single-tier local municipalities in the 2022 results file. "Small" means a 2021 Census population below 30,000. That upper boundary is borrowed from Statistics Canada's small-population-centre category; it is a study definition, not an official municipal class. The resulting group contains 352 municipalities. All remain in the downloadable audit list, but rows with missing or zero ballots are not ranked. [Ontario results] [AMO / 2021 Census population] [Statistics Canada size groups]

The strict comparison uses Ontario's successful-candidate files to keep only municipalities whose mayor or reeve is recorded as Elected, rather than Acclaimed, in both 2018 and 2022. It is still municipality-wide turnout, but it reduces one obvious comparability problem: residents may have had no head-of-council contest on their ballot.

The 2018 source conflict is disclosed, not hidden

Ontario's older raw 2018 export and AMO's published 2018 workbook disagree for many municipalities: 174 elector counts and 58 voter counts differ. For Ramara, the raw Ontario row lists 10,881 electors and would imply 39.92%; AMO's published workbook uses 11,146 electors, 4,344 voters and 38.97%. This study uses AMO's published 2018 rate consistently, with one documented repair for Gananoque where AMO recorded zero voters despite an elected mayor. The workbook's Data Quality sheet shows the reconciliation.

What better participation would look like

The 2022 small-municipality median was 37.40%. At Ramara's 2022 elector count, 157 additional ballots would have moved turnout just above that line. That is an illustration using the 2022 denominator, not a forecast for 2026: the voters' list, contests and comparison group will change.

Turnout is not a score for any candidate. It is one measure of whether residents are participating in the decisions closest to home. For dates, eligibility and official voting information, use the Township of Ramara's election page.
