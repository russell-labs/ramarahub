Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about what Ramara Hub is, who runs it, and how it works. Still have a question? Email russellcolevop@gmail.com.

Is this the Township's official website? Is the mayor involved?

No. Ramara Hub is an independent, free community resource — a project of the Ramara Civic Forum, a resident group. It is not the Township of Ramara's official website, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or speaking for the Township, its council, or the mayor.

The official township website is ramara.ca ↗. The Hub simply gathers public township information into one place and links every answer back to its official source, so you can check it yourself. For anything official — a service request, a complaint, a permit — contact the Township directly (see below).

Who runs Ramara Hub?

Russell Cole, a Ramara resident, built and maintains the site himself. He founded the Ramara Civic Forum after the December 2025 wastewater forums to give residents a standing place to be heard, sits on the board of the Concerned Citizens of Ramara, and was a 2022 candidate for mayor of Ramara. He's been working on how this township runs for years.

Questions about the site go straight to him: russellcolevop@gmail.com. Read more on the About page.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

Completely free, and there's no catch. No accounts, no charge, no ads. It's a volunteer community project — built to make Ramara a little easier to navigate.

What does it cost to run, and how is it funded?

Not much, and that's on purpose. Ramara Hub runs on inexpensive modern tools for around $20 a month. It's reader-supported and independent: not funded by the Township, not funded by advertisers, and there are no ads. That independence is deliberate. It's what lets the site answer questions straight, including about the Township itself. If that ever changes, you'll read it here first.

Where do the answers come from? Can I trust them?

Behind the search bar sits a growing archive of over 900 public township documents — bylaws, council highlights, studies, plans, and service pages collected from ramara.ca — searched live every time. Every result links back to the official document so you can verify it.

Everything published here has to clear one bar: every claim is sourced to a primary or credible published record. If it can't be sourced, it isn't said. See exactly how the Hub decides what to publish.

Will this be kept up to date, or will it go stale?

Yes, and it's built to stay current with very little hand-holding. A lot of the upkeep runs automatically: the site pulls in new Township news each day, keeps its document archive fresh, drafts replies to questions residents leave (a person reviews them before any reply goes out), and flags gaps to be filled. That automation is the point. The Hub keeps working day to day without depending on someone manually tending it, so it won't quietly fall out of date.

Can the person who runs this control the narrative?

Fair question, and the site is built specifically so the answer is no. The 900-plus documents behind it aren't hand-picked — they're pulled automatically from the Township's own website, and the deeper issue briefs draw on outside records too, court rulings and independent journalism. Every answer links straight back to its original source so you can check it yourself. If an answer ever doesn't match its source, that's a bug, and it gets fixed, usually the same day.

The rules are public: facts only, every claim sourced, both sides and the real numbers on contested issues, no grades, no endorsements, and when there's no sourced answer the site says so instead of guessing. We're also not hiding who runs it. The About page says plainly that Russell Cole built and maintains the site and ran for mayor in 2022.

The honest part: any resource involves choices about what to include, and we won't pretend otherwise. The safeguard isn't a claim of perfect neutrality. It's that everything is sourced, everything is checkable, and anyone can flag something to be fixed or added. Holding the site to that standard is exactly how it stays honest.

What guardrails keep the answer engine honest?

The answer engine is designed to answer from retrieved records, not from memory. It should link back to the sources it used, label whose record each source came from, avoid guessing, and make uncertainty visible when the record is incomplete or one-sided.

Those guardrails do not make the Hub perfect. They make it checkable. Corrections and meaningful changes are logged on What's new, and the site code is public on GitHub ↗ so people can inspect how the site changes.

Doesn't the Township control the source data? Couldn't that bias the answers?

An honest yes-and-no. The everyday answers are built mostly from documents the Township itself publishes, because that's what's publicly available to collect. So where the public record is incomplete, the Hub inherits that gap — it can only point you to what exists.

Council minutes are summaries, not transcripts, and public meeting audio and video are not posted as searchable transcripts. The CivicWeb material is not fully in the Hub's searchable archive yet either. We've asked the Township Clerk for those records so they can be added if they are provided.

That's exactly why the deeper issue briefs go outside Township-controlled sources. They're built from court rulings and independent journalism, not the Township's own framing. The clearest example is the shore wall brief: it lays out the Ontario Superior Court's finding that the Township acted in bad faith, and the later contempt finding against it — sourced to the court record and to OrilliaMatters and BarrieToday. A tool spun to favour council wouldn't surface that.

We also name what's missing instead of papering over it. The CivicWeb council-minutes archive isn't in the document set yet, and we've formally asked the Township for the full minutes record so it can be complete. Some council business is handled in closed (in-camera) sessions, which municipalities are allowed to do but which keeps it off the public record. Where the record is thin, withheld, or one-sided, the site says so rather than filling the gap with any one party's version — and it won't state as fact anything it can't source.

Do you track me? What about my privacy?

When you ask a question, the Hub records the question — never who asked it. No accounts, no ads, and no advertising trackers. Question counts tell us what to research next and what to raise with the township.

The site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics for aggregate visit counts. Cloudflare says Web Analytics does not use cookies, localStorage, or fingerprinting to collect usage metrics: Cloudflare Web Analytics ↗.

If you choose to leave your email — for a follow-up answer or the weekly update — it's used for exactly that and nothing else, and you can ask to be removed anytime.

Does my question actually get seen?

Yes. Every question asked here is counted anonymously. The issues raised most often, with the biggest impact on residents, get researched into full issue briefs and brought to the township through the Ramara Civic Forum — the resident group built to do exactly that. Asking a question here is a small act of making Ramara work better.

I found a mistake, or something's missing. What do I do?

Tell us and it gets fixed or added — that's how this site grows. Reach the Ramara Civic Forum through our Facebook group ↗, or email russellcolevop@gmail.com.

How do I get updates?

Sign up for the free weekly email — what neighbours asked, what's new on the Hub, and what's moving at the township. You can add the topics you care about and we'll flag what's relevant to you. Subscribe from the Township news page or the footer of any page. No spam, unsubscribe anytime.

How do I contact the actual Township of Ramara?

Call the Township at 705-484-5374 (Mon–Fri 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m.), use the official Report a Concern portal ↗, or visit ramara.ca ↗. For emergencies, call 911.
